Which colours feel safe?

Same three screens (unlock, password list, password detail) in four colour directions. Layout is fixed from wireframes v1; only colour changes. Labels are plain on purpose while wording is revised. Wordmark has no dot.

Recommendation

Option D: keep Ink + Amber as the dark theme, add a Linen light theme, follow the phone setting

  • Primary users are technical, and they expect dark. Most run dark IDEs and terminals and set their phone to dark. Following the system gives them today's ink + amber palette, identical to the extension, with zero extension work.
  • Calm comes from restraint, not a new hue. In my view the “not safe” feel comes from too much amber on near-black. Use amber for one main action per screen, keep the vignette faint, and use no sweep glow. Switching to navy/teal (C) would cost the brand for a similar gain.
  • Light adds calm where it matters. Daylight and outdoor reading are better on light, and relatives who open the app in an emergency get the paper-like Linen look. Brand stays: amber fills the main button, and a deeper amber-brown for links and icons passes AA (5.6:1).
  • Runner-up: C “Night Navy + Soft Teal” if the owner feels amber itself is the problem. It is calm and reads as security, and stays dark, but the extension's ink and accent must change. B drops to third because its main strength serves the secondary audience.

Trust colours

Blue and teal are the usual trust and security colours in finance apps. Green means “OK / safe”, red means loss or danger, so keep red for errors only. Users expect their category's conventions (ABP 2025, Progress). Evidence is mostly practitioner writing, not controlled studies.

Password managers

Bitwarden: blue #175DDC with deep navy, palette revised 2025–26. 1Password refresh: blues plus a warm beige called “Biscuit”. Proton Pass: purple fading to orange, dark-first app. Google Password Manager: Material You, follows the system theme. All except Proton default to light or follow the system.

Banks (incl. Vietnam)

VCB Digibank launched an “An Vui” mode for older customers in Oct 2024 with large text and clear numbers (Lao Động). It also promotes dark mode as an option. Older users get a mode they pick, not a dark app forced on them.

Dark vs light, older eyes

Piepenbrock et al. (summarised by NN/g): light mode gave better acuity and proofreading for ages 18–33 and 60–85. Some low-vision users, e.g. with cataracts, prefer dark. Technical, dark-habituated users expect dark; light still helps in daylight and for occasional readers. So: support both and follow the system, with dark as the primary design target.

WCAG 2.2 AA

Text needs 4.5:1, large text 3:1, and controls and focus indicators 3:1 against neighbours (1.4.11). Every option below is checked. Amber as text on light fails at 2.0:1, so D uses a separate deeper “accent-ink” for anything that is text or an icon.
